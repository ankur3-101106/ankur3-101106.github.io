Averages and Progressions​

1. Average​

1.1 Fundamental Concept of Average​

1.1.1 Definition and Formula​

The average (or arithmetic mean) of a set of observations is defined as the total sum of all values divided by the total number of values: Average=Sum of QuantitiesNumber of Quantities=∑i=1nxin\text{Average} = \frac{\text{Sum of Quantities}}{\text{Number of Quantities}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n}Average=Number of QuantitiesSum of Quantities​=n∑i=1n​xi​​ Sum of Quantities=Average×Number of Quantities\text{Sum of Quantities} = \text{Average} \times \text{Number of Quantities}Sum of Quantities=Average×Number of Quantities

1.1.2 Weighted Average and Combined Groups​

  • Combined Average of Two Groups: If a group of n1n_1n1​ items has an average of xxx, and a second group of n2n_2n2​ items has an average of yyy, the combined average is: Xˉcomb=n1x+n2yn1+n2\bar{X}_{\text{comb}} = \frac{n_1 x + n_2 y}{n_1 + n_2}Xˉcomb​=n1​+n2​n1​x+n2​y​
  • Average of Remaining Group: If the average of n1n_1n1​ total items is xxx, and a subset of n2n_2n2​ items has an average of yyy, the average of the remaining (n1−n2)(n_1 - n_2)(n1​−n2​) items is: Xˉrem=n1x−n2yn1−n2\bar{X}_{\text{rem}} = \frac{n_1 x - n_2 y}{n_1 - n_2}Xˉrem​=n1​−n2​n1​x−n2​y​

1.2 Mathematical Properties of Averages​

1.2.1 Linear Transformations of Data Sets​

Let xˉ\bar{x}xˉ be the average of x1,x2,…,xnx_1, x_2, \dots, x_nx1​,x2​,…,xn​:

  1. Addition: If each observation is increased by a constant aaa, the new average is: xˉnew=xˉ+a\bar{x}_{\text{new}} = \bar{x} + axˉnew​=xˉ+a
  2. Subtraction: If each observation is decreased by a constant aaa, the new average is: xˉnew=xˉ−a\bar{x}_{\text{new}} = \bar{x} - axˉnew​=xˉ−a
  3. Multiplication: If each observation is multiplied by a non-zero constant aaa, the new average is: xˉnew=a⋅xˉ\bar{x}_{\text{new}} = a \cdot \bar{x}xˉnew​=a⋅xˉ
  4. Division: If each observation is divided by a non-zero constant aaa, the new average is: xˉnew=xˉa\bar{x}_{\text{new}} = \frac{\bar{x}}{a}xˉnew​=axˉ​

1.3 Key Problem Patterns in Averages​

1.3.1 Replacement and Deviation Method​

When a person/item is replaced by a new person/item: Value of New Person=Value of Replaced Person±(Total Persons×Change in Average)\text{Value of New Person} = \text{Value of Replaced Person} \pm (\text{Total Persons} \times \text{Change in Average})Value of New Person=Value of Replaced Person±(Total Persons×Change in Average)

  • Positive sign if the average increases; negative sign if the average decreases.

1.3.2 Correction of Misread / Wrong Data​

Correct Sum=Incorrect Sum−Wrong Value+Correct Value\text{Correct Sum} = \text{Incorrect Sum} - \text{Wrong Value} + \text{Correct Value}Correct Sum=Incorrect Sum−Wrong Value+Correct Value Correct Average=Correct SumTotal Number of Observations\text{Correct Average} = \frac{\text{Correct Sum}}{\text{Total Number of Observations}}Correct Average=Total Number of ObservationsCorrect Sum​

1.3.3 Consecutive Numbers Average​

  • The average of an odd count of consecutive terms (integers, odd, or even numbers in AP) is always the exact middle term.
  • Difference between the largest and smallest of nnn consecutive odd/even numbers is always: Difference=2(n−1)\text{Difference} = 2(n - 1)Difference=2(n−1)

2. Progressions​

2.1 Sequences and Series Overview​

  • Sequence: An ordered list of numbers following a specific mathematical rule: a1,a2,a3,…,ana_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_na1​,a2​,a3​,…,an​.
  • Series: The algebraic sum of the terms of a sequence: S=a1+a2+a3+⋯+anS = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots + a_nS=a1​+a2​+a3​+⋯+an​
    • A series is finite if it terminates after nnn terms, and infinite if it continues indefinitely.

2.2 Arithmetic Progression (A.P.)​

2.2.1 Definitions and Standard Form​

A sequence is an Arithmetic Progression if the difference between any two successive terms is constant: an+1−an=d(common difference),∀n∈Na_{n+1} - a_n = d \quad (\text{common difference}), \quad \forall n \in \mathbb{N}an+1​−an​=d(common difference),∀n∈N

  • Standard AP form: a,a+d,a+2d,a+3d,…a, a + d, a + 2d, a + 3d, \dotsa,a+d,a+2d,a+3d,…
  • nthn^{\text{th}}nth Term (ana_nan​ or Last Term lll): an=l=a+(n−1)da_n = l = a + (n - 1)dan​=l=a+(n−1)d Where aaa is the first term, ddd is the common difference, and nnn is the number of terms.

2.2.2 Convenient Assumptions of Terms​

  • Three terms in AP: a−d,  a,  a+da - d, \; a, \; a + da−d,a,a+d (Sum =3a= 3a=3a)
  • Four terms in AP: a−3d,  a−d,  a+d,  a+3da - 3d, \; a - d, \; a + d, \; a + 3da−3d,a−d,a+d,a+3d (Common difference =2d= 2d=2d, Sum =4a= 4a=4a)
  • Five terms in AP: a−2d,  a−d,  a,  a+d,  a+2da - 2d, \; a - d, \; a, \; a + d, \; a + 2da−2d,a−d,a,a+d,a+2d (Sum =5a= 5a=5a)

2.2.3 Sum of nnn Terms of an A.P. (SnS_nSn​)​

Sn=n2[2a+(n−1)d]=n2[a+l]S_n = \frac{n}{2} [2a + (n - 1)d] = \frac{n}{2} [a + l]Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d]=2n​[a+l]

2.2.4 Arithmetic Mean (A.M.)​

  • If three numbers a,b,ca, b, ca,b,c are in AP: b=a+c2b = \frac{a + c}{2}b=2a+c​ Here, bbb is the Arithmetic Mean between aaa and ccc.

2.3 Geometric Progression (G.P.)​

2.3.1 Definitions and Standard Form​

A sequence of non-zero numbers is a Geometric Progression if the ratio of any term to its preceding term is constant: an+1an=r(common ratio),∀n∈N\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = r \quad (\text{common ratio}), \quad \forall n \in \mathbb{N}an​an+1​​=r(common ratio),∀n∈N

  • Standard GP form: a,ar,ar2,ar3,…a, ar, ar^2, ar^3, \dotsa,ar,ar2,ar3,…
  • nthn^{\text{th}}nth Term (ana_nan​): an=a⋅rn−1a_n = a \cdot r^{n-1}an​=a⋅rn−1

2.3.2 Symmetric Term Selection​

  • Three terms in GP: ar,  a,  ar\frac{a}{r}, \; a, \; arra​,a,ar (Product =a3= a^3=a3)
  • Four terms in GP: ar3,  ar,  ar,  ar3\frac{a}{r^3}, \; \frac{a}{r}, \; ar, \; ar^3r3a​,ra​,ar,ar3 (Common ratio =r2= r^2=r2, Product =a4= a^4=a4)

2.3.3 Sum of nnn Terms of a G.P. (SnS_nSn​)​

Sn={a(1−rn)1−r=a(rn−1)r−1,if r≠1n⋅a,if r=1S_n = \begin{cases} \dfrac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r} = \dfrac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}, & \text{if } r \neq 1 \\ n \cdot a, & \text{if } r = 1 \end{cases}Sn​=⎩⎨⎧​1−ra(1−rn)​=r−1a(rn−1)​,n⋅a,​if r=1if r=1​

2.3.4 Sum of an Infinite G.P. (S∞S_\inftyS∞​)​

When the common ratio satisfies ∣r∣<1|r| < 1∣r∣<1 (i.e., −1<r<1-1 < r < 1−1<r<1): S∞=a1−rS_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}S∞​=1−ra​

2.3.5 Geometric Mean (G.M.)​

  • Three non-zero numbers a,b,ca, b, ca,b,c are in GP if and only if: b2=ac  ⟹  b=acb^2 = ac \implies b = \sqrt{ac}b2=ac⟹b=ac​ Where bbb is the Geometric Mean of aaa and ccc.

2.4 Harmonic Progression (H.P.)​

2.4.1 Definition​

A sequence a1,a2,a3,…,ana_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_na1​,a2​,a3​,…,an​ is in Harmonic Progression if their reciprocals form an Arithmetic Progression: 1a1,1a2,1a3,…,1an are in A.P.\frac{1}{a_1}, \frac{1}{a_2}, \frac{1}{a_3}, \dots, \frac{1}{a_n} \text{ are in A.P.}a1​1​,a2​1​,a3​1​,…,an​1​ are in A.P.

  • nthn^{\text{th}}nth Term of an H.P.: an=1nth term of corresponding AP=11a1+(n−1)dAPa_n = \frac{1}{n^{\text{th}}\text{ term of corresponding AP}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{a_1} + (n - 1)d_{\text{AP}}}an​=nth term of corresponding AP1​=a1​1​+(n−1)dAP​1​
  • Important Constraint: No term of an HP can be equal to zero (division by zero is undefined).

2.4.2 Harmonic Mean (H.M.)​

If a,b,ca, b, ca,b,c are in HP: b=2aca+cb = \frac{2ac}{a + c}b=a+c2ac​

  • Relation between AM, GM, and HM for positive distinct real numbers: AM≥GM≥HMandGM2=AM×HM\text{AM} \ge \text{GM} \ge \text{HM} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{GM}^2 = \text{AM} \times \text{HM}AM≥GM≥HMandGM2=AM×HM

3. Solved Problems and Exam Practice​

Problem 1: Combined Group Average​

Question: The average weight of 242424 students of section A is 58 kg58\text{ kg}58 kg, whereas the average weight of 262626 students of section B is 60.5 kg60.5\text{ kg}60.5 kg. Find the average weight of all 505050 students.

  • Solution: n1=24,x=58,n2=26,y=60.5n_1 = 24, \quad x = 58, \quad n_2 = 26, \quad y = 60.5n1​=24,x=58,n2​=26,y=60.5 Xˉcomb=n1x+n2yn1+n2=(24×58)+(26×60.5)24+26=1392+157350=296550=59.3 kg\bar{X}_{\text{comb}} = \frac{n_1 x + n_2 y}{n_1 + n_2} = \frac{(24 \times 58) + (26 \times 60.5)}{24 + 26} = \frac{1392 + 1573}{50} = \frac{2965}{50} = 59.3\text{ kg}Xˉcomb​=n1​+n2​n1​x+n2​y​=24+26(24×58)+(26×60.5)​=501392+1573​=502965​=59.3 kg
  • Answer: 59.3 kg59.3\text{ kg}59.3 kg.

Problem 2: Remaining Group Average​

Question: The average salary of all 505050 employees including 555 officers is ₹850850850. If the average salary of the officers is ₹250025002500, find the average salary of the remaining staff.

  • Solution: ntotal=50,xˉtotal=850,nofficers=5,yˉofficers=2500n_{\text{total}} = 50, \quad \bar{x}_{\text{total}} = 850, \quad n_{\text{officers}} = 5, \quad \bar{y}_{\text{officers}} = 2500ntotal​=50,xˉtotal​=850,nofficers​=5,yˉ​officers​=2500 Xˉstaff=(50×850)−(5×2500)50−5=42500−1250045=3000045=₹666.67\bar{X}_{\text{staff}} = \frac{(50 \times 850) - (5 \times 2500)}{50 - 5} = \frac{42500 - 12500}{45} = \frac{30000}{45} = ₹666.67Xˉstaff​=50−5(50×850)−(5×2500)​=4542500−12500​=4530000​=₹666.67
  • Answer: ₹666.67666.67666.67 (or ₹667667667).

Problem 3: Target Average Calculation​

Question: A grocer has sales of ₹643564356435, ₹692769276927, ₹685568556855, ₹723072307230, and ₹656265626562 for 555 consecutive months. How much sale must he have in the 6th month to achieve an average sale of ₹650065006500?

  • Solution: Total required sale for 6 months=6500×6=39000\text{Total required sale for 6 months} = 6500 \times 6 = 39000Total required sale for 6 months=6500×6=39000 Total sale for first 5 months=6435+6927+6855+7230+6562=34009\text{Total sale for first 5 months} = 6435 + 6927 + 6855 + 7230 + 6562 = 34009Total sale for first 5 months=6435+6927+6855+7230+6562=34009 6th month sale=39000−34009=₹4991\text{6th month sale} = 39000 - 34009 = ₹49916th month sale=39000−34009=₹4991
  • Answer: ₹499149914991.

Problem 4: Cricket Average Inning Increment​

Question: The average runs of a cricket player over 101010 innings was 323232. How many runs must he score in his next inning to increase his average by 444?

  • Solution: Initial total runs=32×10=320\text{Initial total runs} = 32 \times 10 = 320Initial total runs=32×10=320 New average=32+4=36,New number of innings=11\text{New average} = 32 + 4 = 36, \quad \text{New number of innings} = 11New average=32+4=36,New number of innings=11 New total runs=36×11=396\text{New total runs} = 36 \times 11 = 396New total runs=36×11=396 Runs in 11th inning=396−320=76\text{Runs in 11th inning} = 396 - 320 = 76Runs in 11th inning=396−320=76
  • Answer: 76 runs76\text{ runs}76 runs.

Problem 5: Misread Entry Correction​

Question: A student's marks were wrongly entered as 838383 instead of 636363. Due to that, the average marks for the class increased by 12\frac{1}{2}21​. Find the number of students in the class.

  • Solution: Excess marks added=83−63=20\text{Excess marks added} = 83 - 63 = 20Excess marks added=83−63=20 Total increase in marks=n×12=20  ⟹  n=40\text{Total increase in marks} = n \times \frac{1}{2} = 20 \implies n = 40Total increase in marks=n×21​=20⟹n=40
  • Answer: 40 students40\text{ students}40 students.

Problem 6: Replacement of a Person​

Question: The average weight of 888 persons increases by 2.5 kg2.5\text{ kg}2.5 kg when a new person comes in place of someone weighing 65 kg65\text{ kg}65 kg. Find the weight of the new person.

  • Solution: Total weight increase=8×2.5=20 kg\text{Total weight increase} = 8 \times 2.5 = 20\text{ kg}Total weight increase=8×2.5=20 kg Weight of new person=65+20=85 kg\text{Weight of new person} = 65 + 20 = 85\text{ kg}Weight of new person=65+20=85 kg
  • Answer: 85 kg85\text{ kg}85 kg.

Problem 7: Consecutive Odd Numbers​

Question: The average of 555 consecutive odd numbers is 616161. What is the difference between the highest and lowest numbers?

  • Solution: For any nnn consecutive odd numbers, the difference between the highest and lowest is always 2(n−1)2(n - 1)2(n−1). Difference=2(5−1)=8\text{Difference} = 2(5 - 1) = 8Difference=2(5−1)=8 (Direct verification: The numbers centered at 616161 are 57,59,61,63,6557, 59, 61, 63, 6557,59,61,63,65. Difference =65−57=8= 65 - 57 = 8=65−57=8.)
  • Answer: 888.

  • Answer: 23rd term23^{\text{rd}}\text{ term}23rd term.

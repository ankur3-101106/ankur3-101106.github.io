1. Ratio and Proportion​

1.1 Fundamentals of Ratio​

1.1.1 Definition and Basic Properties​

A ratio is a comparison between two quantities of the same kind and in the same units by magnitude: Ratio of a to b=ab=a:b(b≠0)\text{Ratio of } a \text{ to } b = \frac{a}{b} = a : b \quad (b \neq 0)Ratio of a to b=ba​=a:b(b=0)

  • Antecedent: The first term aaa.
  • Consequent: The second term bbb.
  • Invariance: A ratio remains unchanged if both antecedent and consequent are multiplied or divided by the same non-zero quantity: ab=a⋅kb⋅k=a/kb/k(k≠0)\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \cdot k}{b \cdot k} = \frac{a / k}{b / k} \quad (k \neq 0)ba​=b⋅ka⋅k​=b/ka/k​(k=0)

1.2 Fundamentals of Proportion​

1.2.1 Definition​

An equality of two ratios constitutes a proportion: a:b=c:d  ⟺  a:b::c:d  ⟺  ab=cda : b = c : d \iff a : b :: c : d \iff \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}a:b=c:d⟺a:b::c:d⟺ba​=dc​

  • Extreme terms: aaa and ddd.
  • Mean terms: bbb and ccc.
  • Fundamental Law of Proportion: Product of Extremes=Product of Means  ⟹  a⋅d=b⋅c\text{Product of Extremes} = \text{Product of Means} \implies a \cdot d = b \cdot cProduct of Extremes=Product of Means⟹a⋅d=b⋅c

1.2.2 Proportional Terms​

  • Fourth Proportional: If a:b=c:da : b = c : da:b=c:d, then ddd is the fourth proportional: d=b⋅cad = \frac{b \cdot c}{a}d=ab⋅c​
  • Third Proportional: If a:b=b:ca : b = b : ca:b=b:c, then ccc is called the third proportional to aaa and bbb: b2=a⋅c  ⟹  c=b2ab^2 = a \cdot c \implies c = \frac{b^2}{a}b2=a⋅c⟹c=ab2​
  • Mean Proportional (Second Proportional): If a:x=x:ba : x = x : ba:x=x:b, then xxx is the mean proportional between aaa and bbb: x2=a⋅b  ⟹  x=a⋅bx^2 = a \cdot b \implies x = \sqrt{a \cdot b}x2=a⋅b⟹x=a⋅b​

    • Example: If A:B=3:4A:B = 3:4A:B=3:4 and B:C=8:9B:C = 8:9B:C=8:9: A:B:C=(3×8):(4×8):(4×9)=24:32:36=6:8:9A:B:C = (3 \times 8) : (4 \times 8) : (4 \times 9) = 24 : 32 : 36 = 6 : 8 : 9A:B:C=(3×8):(4×8):(4×9)=24:32:36=6:8:9

1.2.4 Shift Shortcuts (Adding or Subtracting a Constant)​

If two numbers are in the ratio a:ba : ba:b, and adding/subtracting xxx changes the ratio to c:dc : dc:d:

  • The two original numbers are: First Number=a⋅x(c∼d)∣ad−bc∣,Second Number=b⋅x(c∼d)∣ad−bc∣\text{First Number} = \frac{a \cdot x(c \sim d)}{|ad - bc|}, \quad \text{Second Number} = \frac{b \cdot x(c \sim d)}{|ad - bc|}First Number=∣ad−bc∣a⋅x(c∼d)​,Second Number=∣ad−bc∣b⋅x(c∼d)​

2. Problems on Ages​

2.1 Standard Mathematical Framework​

Let the present age of a person be xxx years:

  1. Age nnn years hence (later / in the future): Future Age=x+n\text{Future Age} = x + nFuture Age=x+n
  2. Age nnn years ago (past): Past Age=x−n\text{Past Age} = x - nPast Age=x−n
  3. If ages are in the ratio a:ba : ba:b, express them as axa xax and bxb xbx.
  4. 1n of the age\frac{1}{n}\text{ of the age}n1​ of the age is represented as xn\frac{x}{n}nx​.
AGE GAP INVARIANCE

The age difference between two individuals remains strictly constant throughout their lifetimes. If person A is 555 years older than person B today, person A will still be exactly 555 years older 505050 years from now.

3. Percentage​

3.1 Core Concepts of Percentage​

3.1.1 Definitions and Conversions​

Percentage means "per hundred" (parts out of 100100100): x%=x100x\% = \frac{x}{100}x%=100x​

  • To convert a fraction ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ to a percentage: Percentage=(ab×100)%\text{Percentage} = \left(\frac{a}{b} \times 100\right)\%Percentage=(ba​×100)% 14=(14×100)%=25%,15=20%\frac{1}{4} = \left(\frac{1}{4} \times 100\right)\% = 25\%, \quad \frac{1}{5} = 20\%41​=(41​×100)%=25%,51​=20%

3.2 Percentage Increase, Decrease, and Expenditure Rules​

3.2.1 Consumption and Expenditure Invariance​

If the price of an essential commodity changes by R%R\%R%:

  • Price Increases by R%R\%R%: To keep the total expenditure constant, consumption must be reduced by: Reduction in Consumption=[R100+R×100]%\text{Reduction in Consumption} = \left[\frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100\right]\%Reduction in Consumption=[100+RR​×100]%
  • Price Decreases by R%R\%R%: To keep the total expenditure constant, consumption can be increased by: Increase in Consumption=[R100−R×100]%\text{Increase in Consumption} = \left[\frac{R}{100 - R} \times 100\right]\%Increase in Consumption=[100−RR​×100]%

3.2.2 Asymmetric Comparison Shortcut​

  • If AAA is R%R\%R% more than BBB, then BBB is less than AAA by: [R100+R×100]%\left[\frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100\right]\%[100+RR​×100]%
  • If AAA is R%R\%R% less than BBB, then BBB is more than AAA by: [R100−R×100]%\left[\frac{R}{100 - R} \times 100\right]\%[100−RR​×100]%

3.3 Population and Depreciation Formulas​

3.3.1 Population Growth (Compound Compounding)​

Let the initial population be PPP, increasing at an annual rate of R%R\%R%:

  1. Population after nnn years: Pn=P(1+R100)nP_n = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^nPn​=P(1+100R​)n
  2. Population nnn years ago: Ppast=P(1+R100)nP_{\text{past}} = \frac{P}{\left(1 + \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n}Ppast​=(1+100R​)nP​

3.3.2 Machine Depreciation (Compound Decay)​

Let the present purchase value of equipment be PPP, depreciating at an annual rate of R%R\%R%:

  1. Value after nnn years: Vn=P(1−R100)nV_n = P \left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right)^nVn​=P(1−100R​)n
  2. Value nnn years ago: Vpast=P(1−R100)nV_{\text{past}} = \frac{P}{\left(1 - \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n}Vpast​=(1−100R​)nP​

3.4 Successive Percentage Change​

If a quantity is first increased/decreased by x%x\%x% and then changed by y%y\%y%, the net effective change is: Net Change=(x+y+x⋅y100)%\text{Net Change} = \left(x + y + \frac{x \cdot y}{100}\right)\%Net Change=(x+y+100x⋅y​)% (Use negative values for percentage decreases).

Problem 1: Chain of Inherited Sum​

Question: A sum of ₹535353 is divided among Rohit, Mohit, and Shobhit such that Rohit gets ₹777 more than Mohit, and Mohit gets ₹888 more than Shobhit. Find the ratio of their shares.

  • Solution: Let Shobhit's share be ₹xxx.
    • Mohit's share =x+8= x + 8=x+8
    • Rohit's share =(x+8)+7=x+15= (x + 8) + 7 = x + 15=(x+8)+7=x+15 Total Sum=(x+15)+(x+8)+x=53  ⟹  3x+23=53  ⟹  3x=30  ⟹  x=10\text{Total Sum} = (x + 15) + (x + 8) + x = 53 \implies 3x + 23 = 53 \implies 3x = 30 \implies x = 10Total Sum=(x+15)+(x+8)+x=53⟹3x+23=53⟹3x=30⟹x=10
    • Shobhit =10= 10=10, Mohit =18= 18=18, Rohit =25= 25=25.
    • Ratio (Rohit : Mohit : Shobhit) =25:18:10= 25 : 18 : 10=25:18:10.
  • Answer: 25:18:1025 : 18 : 1025:18:10.

Question: A student has to obtain 33%33\%33% of total marks to pass. He gets 125125125 marks and fails by 404040 marks. Find the maximum marks.

  • Solution: Minimum Passing Marks=125+40=165\text{Minimum Passing Marks} = 125 + 40 = 165Minimum Passing Marks=125+40=165 33% of Maximum Marks=165  ⟹  33100×M=165  ⟹  M=165×10033=50033\% \text{ of Maximum Marks} = 165 \implies \frac{33}{100} \times M = 165 \implies M = \frac{165 \times 100}{33} = 50033% of Maximum Marks=165⟹10033​×M=165⟹M=33165×100​=500
  • Answer: 500500500.

Problem 6: Successive Price Changes​

Question: If the price of a book is first decreased by 25%25\%25% and then increased by 20%20\%20%, find the net percentage change.

  • Solution: Using successive percentage formula with x=−25x = -25x=−25 and y=+20y = +20y=+20: Net Change=−25+20+(−25)(20)100=−5−5=−10%\text{Net Change} = -25 + 20 + \frac{(-25)(20)}{100} = -5 - 5 = -10\%Net Change=−25+20+100(−25)(20)​=−5−5=−10% The negative sign indicates a net decrease of 10%10\%10%.
  • Answer: 10% decrease10\%\text{ decrease}10% decrease.

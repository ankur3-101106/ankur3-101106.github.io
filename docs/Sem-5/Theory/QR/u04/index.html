1. Profit and Loss​

Commercial transactions revolve around buying and selling goods and services. The fundamental objective in trade is generating profit.

1.1 Core Terminologies​

  • Cost Price (CP\text{CP}CP): The total price paid to acquire an article, including additional overhead costs (freight, transportation, repairs, labor).
  • Selling Price (SP\text{SP}SP): The price at which an article is sold to a customer.
  • Marked Price / List Price (MP\text{MP}MP): The price labeled or printed on the product before discounts.
  • Profit / Gain (PPP): When SP>CP\text{SP} > \text{CP}SP>CP, the seller makes a profit: P=SP−CPP = \text{SP} - \text{CP}P=SP−CP
  • Loss (LLL): When CP>SP\text{CP} > \text{SP}CP>SP, the seller incurs a loss: L=CP−SPL = \text{CP} - \text{SP}L=CP−SP
  • Profit Percentage (Gain%\text{Gain}\%Gain%): Always calculated with respect to Cost Price: Gain%=(PCP)×100=(SP−CPCP)×100\text{Gain}\% = \left(\frac{P}{\text{CP}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}}\right) \times 100Gain%=(CPP​)×100=(CPSP−CP​)×100
  • Loss Percentage (Loss%\text{Loss}\%Loss%): Always calculated with respect to Cost Price: Loss%=(LCP)×100=(CP−SPCP)×100\text{Loss}\% = \left(\frac{L}{\text{CP}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{\text{CP} - \text{SP}}{\text{CP}}\right) \times 100Loss%=(CPL​)×100=(CPCP−SP​)×100

1.2 Fundamental Relationships​

Expressing SP\text{SP}SP in terms of CP\text{CP}CP: SP=CP×(100+Gain%100)orSP=CP×(100−Loss%100)\text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(\frac{100 + \text{Gain}\%}{100}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad \text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(\frac{100 - \text{Loss}\%}{100}\right)SP=CP×(100100+Gain%​)orSP=CP×(100100−Loss%​)

Expressing CP\text{CP}CP in terms of SP\text{SP}SP: CP=SP×(100100+Gain%)orCP=SP×(100100−Loss%)\text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left(\frac{100}{100 + \text{Gain}\%}\right) \quad \text{or} \quad \text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \left(\frac{100}{100 - \text{Loss}\%}\right)CP=SP×(100+Gain%100​)orCP=SP×(100−Loss%100​)

1.3 Discount and Marked Price​

Discount is the reduction given on the Marked Price: Discount=MP−SP\text{Discount} = \text{MP} - \text{SP}Discount=MP−SP Discount%=(DiscountMP)×100\text{Discount}\% = \left(\frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}}\right) \times 100Discount%=(MPDiscount​)×100 SP=MP×(1−D%100)\text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left(1 - \frac{D\%}{100}\right)SP=MP×(1−100D%​)

Successive Discounts​

If successive discounts of d1%,d2%,…,dn%d_1\%, d_2\%, \dots, d_n\%d1​%,d2​%,…,dn​% are given: SP=MP×(1−d1100)×(1−d2100)×⋯×(1−dn100)\text{SP} = \text{MP} \times \left(1 - \frac{d_1}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{d_2}{100}\right) \times \dots \times \left(1 - \frac{d_n}{100}\right)SP=MP×(1−100d1​​)×(1−100d2​​)×⋯×(1−100dn​​)

For two successive discounts of a%a\%a% and b%b\%b%, the single equivalent discount is: Equivalent Discount%=(a+b−a×b100)%\text{Equivalent Discount}\% = \left(a + b - \frac{a \times b}{100}\right)\%Equivalent Discount%=(a+b−100a×b​)%

1.4 Exam Shortcuts and Special Cases​

Case 1: CP of mmm Articles equals SP of nnn Articles​

Gain or Loss%=(m−nn)×100\text{Gain or Loss}\% = \left(\frac{m - n}{n}\right) \times 100Gain or Loss%=(nm−n​)×100

  • If m>nm > nm>n, it is a Gain.
  • If m<nm < nm<n, it is a Loss.

Case 2: Cross-Multiplication Method for Unit Transactions​

If a person buys xxx items for ₹yyy and sells zzz items for ₹www: Profit or Loss%=(x⋅w−z⋅yz⋅y)×100\text{Profit or Loss}\% = \left(\frac{x \cdot w - z \cdot y}{z \cdot y}\right) \times 100Profit or Loss%=(z⋅yx⋅w−z⋅y​)×100

  • A positive result indicates profit, while a negative result indicates loss.

Case 3: Same Selling Price with Equal Gain% and Loss%​

When two different articles are sold at the same selling price (SP1=SP2\text{SP}_1 = \text{SP}_2SP1​=SP2​), one at a gain of x%x\%x% and the other at a loss of x%x\%x%:

  • There is always an overall loss. Overall Loss%=(x10)2%=x2100%\text{Overall Loss}\% = \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)^2 \% = \frac{x^2}{100}\%Overall Loss%=(10x​)2%=100x2​%

Case 4: Dishonest Dealer / False Weights​

When a trader professes to sell at cost price but uses a false weight / false scale: Gain%=(ErrorTrue Value−Error)×100=(True Weight−False WeightFalse Weight)×100\text{Gain}\% = \left(\frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{\text{True Weight} - \text{False Weight}}{\text{False Weight}}\right) \times 100Gain%=(True Value−ErrorError​)×100=(False WeightTrue Weight−False Weight​)×100

Case 5: Consecutive Selling Prices for Unknown CP​

If an article sold at price SP1\text{SP}_1SP1​ yields a gain/loss of r1%r_1\%r1​% and at SP2\text{SP}_2SP2​ yields a gain/loss of r2%r_2\%r2​%: SP1100±r1=SP2100±r2=CP100\frac{\text{SP}_1}{100 \pm r_1} = \frac{\text{SP}_2}{100 \pm r_2} = \frac{\text{CP}}{100}100±r1​SP1​​=100±r2​SP2​​=100CP​

Case 6: Chain of Sales (A→B→CA \to B \to CA→B→C)​

If AAA sells to BBB at a profit of x%x\%x%, and BBB sells to CCC at a profit of y%y\%y%: Cost for C=Cost for A×(1+x100)×(1+y100)\text{Cost for } C = \text{Cost for } A \times \left(1 + \frac{x}{100}\right) \times \left(1 + \frac{y}{100}\right)Cost for C=Cost for A×(1+100x​)×(1+100y​)

2. Partnership​

A partnership is an association of two or more persons who combine capital, labor, or skill to carry on a business and agree to share profits and losses proportionally.

2.1 Types of Partners​

  • Working Partner: A partner who actively manages business operations. Such a partner often receives an agreed salary or percentage allowance from the gross profit before residual profits are split.
  • Sleeping / Dormant Partner: A partner who only contributes financial capital without taking part in daily operations.

2.2 Ratio of Division of Gains​

Simple Partnership (Equal Time)​

When partners invest capital C1,C2,C3,…,CnC_1, C_2, C_3, \dots, C_nC1​,C2​,C3​,…,Cn​ for the exact same duration: Ratio of Profits=C1:C2:C3:⋯:Cn\text{Ratio of Profits} = C_1 : C_2 : C_3 : \dots : C_nRatio of Profits=C1​:C2​:C3​:⋯:Cn​

Compound Partnership (Unequal Time)​

When partners invest capital C1,C2,…C_1, C_2, \dotsC1​,C2​,… for different time periods T1,T2,…T_1, T_2, \dotsT1​,T2​,…: Ratio of Profits=(C1×T1):(C2×T2):(C3×T3):…\text{Ratio of Profits} = (C_1 \times T_1) : (C_2 \times T_2) : (C_3 \times T_3) : \dotsRatio of Profits=(C1​×T1​):(C2​×T2​):(C3​×T3​):…

Individual share of total profit PtotalP_{\text{total}}Ptotal​: Share of Partner A=Ptotal×CATACATA+CBTB+…\text{Share of Partner } A = P_{\text{total}} \times \frac{C_A T_A}{C_A T_A + C_B T_B + \dots}Share of Partner A=Ptotal​×CA​TA​+CB​TB​+…CA​TA​​

2.3 Variable Capital Adjustments​

When partners inject or withdraw capital over the investment cycle: Equivalent Monthly Capital=∑(Capitali×Durationi)\text{Equivalent Monthly Capital} = \sum (\text{Capital}_i \times \text{Duration}_i)Equivalent Monthly Capital=∑(Capitali​×Durationi​) Profit Ratio=(∑CA,iTA,i):(∑CB,iTB,i)\text{Profit Ratio} = \left(\sum C_{A,i} T_{A,i}\right) : \left(\sum C_{B,i} T_{B,i}\right)Profit Ratio=(∑CA,i​TA,i​):(∑CB,i​TB,i​)

3. Simple Interest (S.I) and Compound Interest (C.I)​

3.1 Simple Interest (S.I)​

In Simple Interest, interest is computed uniformly on the initial principal alone throughout the loan tenure.

Formula​

S.I=P×R×T100\text{S.I} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}S.I=100P×R×T​

  • PPP = Principal amount (borrowed or invested sum)
  • RRR = Rate of interest per annum (%\%%)
  • TTT = Time period (in years)

Total Amount (AAA): A=P+S.I=P×(1+R×T100)A = P + \text{S.I} = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R \times T}{100}\right)A=P+S.I=P×(1+100R×T​)

Key Shortcuts for S.I​

  • Sum doubles in TTT years: S.I=P  ⟹  P⋅R⋅T100=P  ⟹  R=100T%andT=100R years\text{S.I} = P \implies \frac{P \cdot R \cdot T}{100} = P \implies R = \frac{100}{T}\% \quad \text{and} \quad T = \frac{100}{R} \text{ years}S.I=P⟹100P⋅R⋅T​=P⟹R=T100​%andT=R100​ years
  • Sum becomes nnn times in TTT years: S.I=(n−1)P  ⟹  R=(n−1)×100T%\text{S.I} = (n - 1)P \implies R = \frac{(n - 1) \times 100}{T}\%S.I=(n−1)P⟹R=T(n−1)×100​%
  • If a sum becomes n1n_1n1​ times in T1T_1T1​ years, it becomes n2n_2n2​ times in T2T_2T2​ years: T1T2=n1−1n2−1\frac{T_1}{T_2} = \frac{n_1 - 1}{n_2 - 1}T2​T1​​=n2​−1n1​−1​

3.2 Compound Interest (C.I)​

In Compound Interest, the interest earned at the end of each conversion period is added to the principal to form the new principal for the subsequent period ("interest on interest").

Standard Annual Compounding Formula​

A=P×(1+R100)TA = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^TA=P×(1+100R​)T C.I=A−P=P×[(1+R100)T−1]\text{C.I} = A - P = P \times \left[\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T - 1\right]C.I=A−P=P×[(1+100R​)T−1]

Compounding Frequencies​

  • Half-Yearly (Semi-Annually): Interest is compounded every 6 months (2 times a year). Rate=R2%,Time=2T  ⟹  A=P×(1+R200)2T\text{Rate} = \frac{R}{2}\%, \quad \text{Time} = 2T \implies A = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R}{200}\right)^{2T}Rate=2R​%,Time=2T⟹A=P×(1+200R​)2T
  • Quarterly: Interest is compounded every 3 months (4 times a year). Rate=R4%,Time=4T  ⟹  A=P×(1+R400)4T\text{Rate} = \frac{R}{4}\%, \quad \text{Time} = 4T \implies A = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R}{400}\right)^{4T}Rate=4R​%,Time=4T⟹A=P×(1+400R​)4T
  • Fractional Time (e.g., T=abcT = a \frac{b}{c}T=acb​ years): A=P×(1+R100)a×(1+bc×R100)A = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^a \times \left(1 + \frac{\frac{b}{c} \times R}{100}\right)A=P×(1+100R​)a×(1+100cb​×R​)
  • Varying Annual Rates (R1%,R2%,R3%R_1\%, R_2\%, R_3\%R1​%,R2​%,R3​% for years 1, 2, and 3): A=P×(1+R1100)(1+R2100)(1+R3100)A = P \times \left(1 + \frac{R_1}{100}\right)\left(1 + \frac{R_2}{100}\right)\left(1 + \frac{R_3}{100}\right)A=P×(1+100R1​​)(1+100R2​​)(1+100R3​​)

3.3 Difference Between C.I and S.I​

For a given principal PPP and annual rate R%R\%R%:

For 2 Years:​

D2=C.I2−S.I2=P×(R100)2D_2 = \text{C.I}_2 - \text{S.I}_2 = P \times \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2D2​=C.I2​−S.I2​=P×(100R​)2

For 3 Years:​

D3=C.I3−S.I3=P×(R100)2×(300+R100)D_3 = \text{C.I}_3 - \text{S.I}_3 = P \times \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \times \left(\frac{300 + R}{100}\right)D3​=C.I3​−S.I3​=P×(100R​)2×(100300+R​)

D3D2=300+R100=3+R100\frac{D_3}{D_2} = \frac{300 + R}{100} = 3 + \frac{R}{100}D2​D3​​=100300+R​=3+100R​

4. Solved Problems and Exam Practice​

Problem 1 (Profit Calculation)​

Mr. Sharma buys a cooler for ₹4,500. For how much should he sell it to gain 8%?

Solution: CP=4500,Gain%=8%\text{CP} = 4500, \quad \text{Gain}\% = 8\%CP=4500,Gain%=8% SP=CP×(100+8100)=4500×1.08=4860\text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(\frac{100 + 8}{100}\right) = 4500 \times 1.08 = 4860SP=CP×(100100+8​)=4500×1.08=4860 He should sell the cooler for ₹4,860.

Problem 2 (Selling Price to Cost Price Conversion)​

A man sold a radio for ₹1,980 at a gain of 10%. What was the cost price?

Solution: CP=SP×100100+Gain%=1980×100110=1980×1011=1800\text{CP} = \text{SP} \times \frac{100}{100 + \text{Gain}\%} = 1980 \times \frac{100}{110} = 1980 \times \frac{10}{11} = 1800CP=SP×100+Gain%100​=1980×110100​=1980×1110​=1800 The cost price is ₹1,800.

Problem 3 (Cross-Multiplication Unit Transaction)​

A fruit vendor buys lemons at the rate of 6 for ₹10 and sells them at 4 for ₹8. Find his profit or loss percentage.

Solution: Using the cross-multiplication formula:

  • Bought: x=6x = 6x=6 items for y=10y = 10y=10
  • Sold: z=4z = 4z=4 items for w=8w = 8w=8 Gain%=(x⋅w−z⋅yz⋅y)×100=(6×8−4×104×10)×100=(48−4040)×100=840×100=20%\text{Gain}\% = \left(\frac{x \cdot w - z \cdot y}{z \cdot y}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{6 \times 8 - 4 \times 10}{4 \times 10}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{48 - 40}{40}\right) \times 100 = \frac{8}{40} \times 100 = 20\%Gain%=(z⋅yx⋅w−z⋅y​)×100=(4×106×8−4×10​)×100=(4048−40​)×100=408​×100=20% The vendor gains 20%.

A dishonest dealer sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 grams instead of a 1 kg weight. What is his percentage profit?

Solution: Error=1000 g−900 g=100 g\text{Error} = 1000\text{ g} - 900\text{ g} = 100\text{ g}Error=1000 g−900 g=100 g Gain%=(ErrorTrue Weight−Error)×100=(100900)×100=1009%=1119%\text{Gain}\% = \left(\frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Weight} - \text{Error}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{100}{900}\right) \times 100 = \frac{100}{9}\% = 11\frac{1}{9}\%Gain%=(True Weight−ErrorError​)×100=(900100​)×100=9100​%=1191​%

A shopkeeper sells two television sets at ₹9,900 each. On one he gains 10%, and on the other he loses 10%. Find his overall profit or loss percentage.

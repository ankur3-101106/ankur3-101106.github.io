1. Time and Work​

Time and work problems model the relationship between the workforce, work done, and time taken. Work and time are inversely proportional when efficiency and workforce are held constant.

1.1 Fundamental Principles​

  • Work as a Whole: Complete work is conventionally quantified as 111 unit (or 100%100\%100%).
  • Per Day's Work: If a person completes an entire piece of work in nnn days: Work done in 1 day=1n\text{Work done in 1 day} = \frac{1}{n}Work done in 1 day=n1​ Conversely, if 1 day's work is 1n\frac{1}{n}n1​, the total time to finish the work is nnn days.
  • Combined Work (Two Entities): If person AAA finishes work in xxx days and person BBB in yyy days: Work done by (A+B) in 1 day=1x+1y=x+yxy\text{Work done by } (A + B) \text{ in 1 day} = \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} = \frac{x + y}{xy}Work done by (A+B) in 1 day=x1​+y1​=xyx+y​ Total time taken together=xyx+y days\text{Total time taken together} = \frac{xy}{x + y} \text{ days}Total time taken together=x+yxy​ days
  • Combined Work (Three Entities): If A,B,CA, B, CA,B,C finish work in x,y,zx, y, zx,y,z days respectively: Work done by (A+B+C) in 1 day=1x+1y+1z=xy+yz+zxxyz\text{Work done by } (A + B + C) \text{ in 1 day} = \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{z} = \frac{xy + yz + zx}{xyz}Work done by (A+B+C) in 1 day=x1​+y1​+z1​=xyzxy+yz+zx​ Total time taken together=xyzxy+yz+zx days\text{Total time taken together} = \frac{xyz}{xy + yz + zx} \text{ days}Total time taken together=xy+yz+zxxyz​ days

1.2 The LCM (Total Units) Method​

Rather than manipulating fractions, assume total work to be the LCM\text{LCM}LCM of the individual days:

  1. Total Work (in units)=LCM(d1,d2,…,dk)\text{Total Work (in units)} = \text{LCM}(d_1, d_2, \dots, d_k)Total Work (in units)=LCM(d1​,d2​,…,dk​).
  2. Calculate individual daily efficiency: Efficiency Ei=Total Work Unitsdi\text{Efficiency } E_i = \frac{\text{Total Work Units}}{d_i}Efficiency Ei​=di​Total Work Units​
  3. Total combined daily efficiency: Enet=∑EiE_{\text{net}} = \sum E_iEnet​=∑Ei​
  4. Total days required: Days=Total Work UnitsEnet\text{Days} = \frac{\text{Total Work Units}}{E_{\text{net}}}Days=Enet​Total Work Units​

1.3 Efficiency and Wages​

  • Efficiency-Time Invariance: Efficiency is inversely proportional to time taken: E1E2=T2T1\frac{E_1}{E_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1}E2​E1​​=T1​T2​​ If AAA is kkk times as efficient as BBB, then AAA takes 1k\frac{1}{k}k1​ of the time taken by BBB.
  • Wages Distribution: When individuals work for the same duration, wages are distributed strictly in the ratio of their efficiencies (or work completed): Ratio of Wages=E1:E2:⋯:En=1T1:1T2:⋯:1Tn\text{Ratio of Wages} = E_1 : E_2 : \dots : E_n = \frac{1}{T_1} : \frac{1}{T_2} : \dots : \frac{1}{T_n}Ratio of Wages=E1​:E2​:⋯:En​=T1​1​:T2​1​:⋯:Tn​1​

1.4 The General Chain Rule (M−D−H−WM-D-H-WM−D−H−W)​

If M1M_1M1​ persons work D1D_1D1​ days for H1H_1H1​ hours per day with efficiency E1E_1E1​ to complete W1W_1W1​ amount of work, and M2M_2M2​ persons work D2D_2D2​ days for H2H_2H2​ hours per day with efficiency E2E_2E2​ to complete W2W_2W2​ work: M1⋅D1⋅H1⋅E1W1=M2⋅D2⋅H2⋅E2W2\frac{M_1 \cdot D_1 \cdot H_1 \cdot E_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 \cdot D_2 \cdot H_2 \cdot E_2}{W_2}W1​M1​⋅D1​⋅H1​⋅E1​​=W2​M2​⋅D2​⋅H2​⋅E2​​

2. Pipes and Cisterns​

Pipes and cisterns problems are mathematically identical to time and work, with the addition of negative work done by drain or leak pipes.

2.1 Core Concepts​

  • Inlet Pipe: Fills the tank (performs positive work). If an inlet fills a tank in xxx hours, part filled in 1 hour =+1x= +\frac{1}{x}=+x1​.
  • Outlet / Leak Pipe: Empties the tank (performs negative work). If an outlet empties a tank in yyy hours, part emptied in 1 hour =−1y= -\frac{1}{y}=−y1​.
  • Net Work Rate: If an inlet fills the tank in xxx hours and an outlet empties it in yyy hours (y>xy > xy>x): Net part filled in 1 hour=1x−1y=y−xxy\text{Net part filled in 1 hour} = \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y} = \frac{y - x}{xy}Net part filled in 1 hour=x1​−y1​=xyy−x​ Time taken to fill the tank completely=xyy−x hours\text{Time taken to fill the tank completely} = \frac{xy}{y - x} \text{ hours}Time taken to fill the tank completely=y−xxy​ hours
  • If x>yx > yx>y, the net rate is negative and the tank is drained in: Time to empty full tank=xyx−y hours\text{Time to empty full tank} = \frac{xy}{x - y} \text{ hours}Time to empty full tank=x−yxy​ hours

3. Time, Speed and Distance (TSD)​

3.1 Fundamental Equation and Conversions​

Distance (D)=Speed (S)×Time (T)\text{Distance } (D) = \text{Speed } (S) \times \text{Time } (T)Distance (D)=Speed (S)×Time (T) S=DT,T=DSS = \frac{D}{T}, \quad T = \frac{D}{S}S=TD​,T=SD​

Unit Conversion Factors​

  • km/h\text{km/h}km/h to m/s\text{m/s}m/s: 1 km/h=1000 m3600 s=518 m/s1 \text{ km/h} = \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{3600 \text{ s}} = \frac{5}{18} \text{ m/s}1 km/h=3600 s1000 m​=185​ m/s Multiply by 518\frac{5}{18}185​ to convert km/h→m/s\text{km/h} \to \text{m/s}km/h→m/s.
  • m/s\text{m/s}m/s to km/h\text{km/h}km/h: 1 m/s=185 km/h1 \text{ m/s} = \frac{18}{5} \text{ km/h}1 m/s=518​ km/h Multiply by 185\frac{18}{5}518​ to convert m/s→km/h\text{m/s} \to \text{km/h}m/s→km/h.

3.2 Proportionality Rules​

  • Constant Distance (DDD constant): Speed is inversely proportional to time (S∝1TS \propto \frac{1}{T}S∝T1​): S1S2=T2T1\frac{S_1}{S_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1}S2​S1​​=T1​T2​​
  • Constant Time (TTT constant): Distance is directly proportional to speed (D∝SD \propto SD∝S): D1D2=S1S2\frac{D_1}{D_2} = \frac{S_1}{S_2}D2​D1​​=S2​S1​​
  • Constant Speed (SSS constant): Distance is directly proportional to time (D∝TD \propto TD∝T): D1D2=T1T2\frac{D_1}{D_2} = \frac{T_1}{T_2}D2​D1​​=T2​T1​​

3.3 Average Speed​

Average Speed=Total Distance TraveledTotal Time Taken\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance Traveled}}{\text{Total Time Taken}}Average Speed=Total Time TakenTotal Distance Traveled​

  • Equal Distances: If a body covers a distance at speed xxx and returns the same distance at speed yyy: Average Speed=2xyx+y\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x + y}Average Speed=x+y2xy​
  • Three Equal Distances: Traveled at speeds x,y,zx, y, zx,y,z: Average Speed=3xyzxy+yz+zx\text{Average Speed} = \frac{3xyz}{xy + yz + zx}Average Speed=xy+yz+zx3xyz​
  • Equal Time Intervals: If a body travels for equal durations at speeds xxx and yyy: Average Speed=x+y2\text{Average Speed} = \frac{x + y}{2}Average Speed=2x+y​

5. Boats and Streams​

When water moves with a velocity of its own, it aids or opposes the motion of a boat or swimmer.

5.1 Basic Definitions​

  • Let uuu = Speed of the boat in still water (km/h\text{km/h}km/h).
  • Let vvv = Speed of the water current / stream (km/h\text{km/h}km/h).

5.2 Downstream and Upstream Speeds​

  • Downstream Speed (DDD): Motion along the direction of water flow: D=u+vD = u + vD=u+v
  • Upstream Speed (UUU): Motion opposing the direction of water flow: U=u−v(u>v)U = u - v \quad (u > v)U=u−v(u>v)

5.3 Calculating Boat and Stream Speeds​

Given downstream speed DDD and upstream speed UUU: Speed of Boat in Still Water (u)=D+U2\text{Speed of Boat in Still Water } (u) = \frac{D + U}{2}Speed of Boat in Still Water (u)=2D+U​ Speed of Stream (v)=D−U2\text{Speed of Stream } (v) = \frac{D - U}{2}Speed of Stream (v)=2D−U​

5.4 Round Trip Shortcuts​

  • If a boat travels a distance ddd downstream and returns the same distance ddd upstream in total time TTT: T=du+v+du−v=2duu2−v2T = \frac{d}{u + v} + \frac{d}{u - v} = \frac{2du}{u^2 - v^2}T=u+vd​+u−vd​=u2−v22du​ d=T(u2−v2)2ud = \frac{T(u^2 - v^2)}{2u}d=2uT(u2−v2)​
  • If the time taken upstream is kkk times the time taken downstream for the same distance: u+vu−v=k  ⟹  u=v(k+1k−1)\frac{u + v}{u - v} = k \implies u = v \left(\frac{k + 1}{k - 1}\right)u−vu+v​=k⟹u=v(k−1k+1​)

6. Solved Problems and Exam Practice​

Problem 1 (Time and Work - LCM Method)​

AAA can do a piece of work in 10 days, and BBB can do it in 15 days. How many days will they take to complete the work together?

Solution:

  1. Assume total work units =LCM(10,15)=30= \text{LCM}(10, 15) = 30=LCM(10,15)=30 units.
  2. Efficiency of A=3010=3A = \frac{30}{10} = 3A=1030​=3 units/day.
  3. Efficiency of B=3015=2B = \frac{30}{15} = 2B=1530​=2 units/day.
  4. Combined efficiency =3+2=5= 3 + 2 = 5=3+2=5 units/day. Time taken=305=6 days\text{Time taken} = \frac{30}{5} = 6 \text{ days}Time taken=530​=6 days

Problem 2 (Work with Leaving Worker)​

AAA can do a work in 14 days and BBB in 21 days. They begin together, but 3 days before the completion of the work, AAA leaves off. What is the total time taken to complete the work?

Solution: Total work units =LCM(14,21)=42= \text{LCM}(14, 21) = 42=LCM(14,21)=42 units.

  • Efficiency of A=4214=3A = \frac{42}{14} = 3A=1442​=3 units/day.
  • Efficiency of B=4221=2B = \frac{42}{21} = 2B=2142​=2 units/day.

In the last 3 days, BBB works alone: Work done by B in last 3 days=3×2=6 units\text{Work done by } B \text{ in last 3 days} = 3 \times 2 = 6 \text{ units}Work done by B in last 3 days=3×2=6 units Remaining work completed by (A+B) together=42−6=36 units\text{Remaining work completed by } (A + B) \text{ together} = 42 - 6 = 36 \text{ units}Remaining work completed by (A+B) together=42−6=36 units Days worked together=363+2=365=7.2 days\text{Days worked together} = \frac{36}{3 + 2} = \frac{36}{5} = 7.2 \text{ days}Days worked together=3+236​=536​=7.2 days Total time=7.2+3=10.2 days=1015 days\text{Total time} = 7.2 + 3 = 10.2 \text{ days} = 10\frac{1}{5} \text{ days}Total time=7.2+3=10.2 days=1051​ days

Problem 3 (Efficiency Comparison)​

AAA is twice as fast as BBB. If BBB alone can complete a piece of work in 30 days, in what time can AAA and BBB together complete the work?

Solution:

  • Since AAA is twice as fast as BBB, AAA takes half the time of BBB: Time for A=302=15 days\text{Time for } A = \frac{30}{2} = 15 \text{ days}Time for A=230​=15 days
  • Total work =LCM(15,30)=30= \text{LCM}(15, 30) = 30=LCM(15,30)=30 units.
  • Efficiency of A=2A = 2A=2 units/day; efficiency of B=1B = 1B=1 unit/day.
  • Combined efficiency =2+1=3= 2 + 1 = 3=2+1=3 units/day. Time taken together=303=10 days\text{Time taken together} = \frac{30}{3} = 10 \text{ days}Time taken together=330​=10 days

Problem 4 (Pipes and Cisterns with Leak)​

A pipe can fill a tank in 10 hours. Due to a leak in the bottom, it fills in 12 hours. If the tank is full, in how many hours will the leak empty it?

Solution: Rate of pipe P=+110\text{Rate of pipe } P = +\frac{1}{10}Rate of pipe P=+101​ Net rate of pipe and leak (P−L)=+112\text{Net rate of pipe and leak } (P - L) = +\frac{1}{12}Net rate of pipe and leak (P−L)=+121​ Rate of leak L=110−112=6−560=160\text{Rate of leak } L = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{6 - 5}{60} = \frac{1}{60}Rate of leak L=101​−121​=606−5​=601​ The leak will empty the full tank in 60 hours.

Problem 5 (Average Speed over Equal Distances)​

A car covers a distance of 120 km at 40 km/h and returns the same distance at 60 km/h. Find the average speed of the car for the entire journey.

Solution: Since the forward and return distances are equal: Average Speed=2xyx+y=2×40×6040+60=4800100=48 km/h\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x + y} = \frac{2 \times 40 \times 60}{40 + 60} = \frac{4800}{100} = 48 \text{ km/h}Average Speed=x+y2xy​=40+602×40×60​=1004800​=48 km/h

Problem 6 (Train Crossing a Stationary Platform)​

A train 150 meters long is traveling at a speed of 54 km/h. How much time will it take to pass a railway platform 250 meters in length?

Solution:

  1. Convert speed to m/s: S=54×518=15 m/sS = 54 \times \frac{5}{18} = 15 \text{ m/s}S=54×185​=15 m/s
  2. Total distance to be cleared: D=Ltrain+Lplatform=150+250=400 mD = L_{\text{train}} + L_{\text{platform}} = 150 + 250 = 400 \text{ m}D=Ltrain​+Lplatform​=150+250=400 m
  3. Time taken: T=DS=40015=803=26.67 secondsT = \frac{D}{S} = \frac{400}{15} = \frac{80}{3} = 26.67 \text{ seconds}T=SD​=15400​=380​=26.67 seconds

Problem 7 (Two Trains Crossing in Opposite Directions)​

Two trains of length 140 m and 160 m run on parallel tracks in opposite directions with speeds of 60 km/h and 48 km/h respectively. In what time will they clear each other?

Solution:

  1. Total distance: D=L1+L2=140+160=300 mD = L_1 + L_2 = 140 + 160 = 300 \text{ m}D=L1​+L2​=140+160=300 m
  2. Relative speed (opposite directions): Srel=60+48=108 km/hS_{\text{rel}} = 60 + 48 = 108 \text{ km/h}Srel​=60+48=108 km/h
  3. Convert to m/s: Srel=108×518=6×5=30 m/sS_{\text{rel}} = 108 \times \frac{5}{18} = 6 \times 5 = 30 \text{ m/s}Srel​=108×185​=6×5=30 m/s
  4. Time taken: T=30030=10 secondsT = \frac{300}{30} = 10 \text{ seconds}T=30300​=10 seconds

Problem 8 (Two Trains Crossing in the Same Direction)​

Two trains 100 m and 120 m long are running in the same direction on parallel tracks. The faster train runs at 72 km/h and the slower train at 54 km/h. In what time will the faster train completely pass the slower one?

Solution:

  1. Total distance: D=100+120=220 mD = 100 + 120 = 220 \text{ m}D=100+120=220 m
  2. Relative speed: Srel=72−54=18 km/h=18×518=5 m/sS_{\text{rel}} = 72 - 54 = 18 \text{ km/h} = 18 \times \frac{5}{18} = 5 \text{ m/s}Srel​=72−54=18 km/h=18×185​=5 m/s
  3. Time taken: T=2205=44 secondsT = \frac{220}{5} = 44 \text{ seconds}T=5220​=44 seconds

Problem 9 (Boats and Streams Speed Determination)​

A man can row upstream at 16 km/h and downstream at 26 km/h. Find his rowing speed in still water and the rate of the water current.

Solution: Given D=26 km/hD = 26 \text{ km/h}D=26 km/h and U=16 km/hU = 16 \text{ km/h}U=16 km/h: Speed in still water (u)=D+U2=26+162=422=21 km/h\text{Speed in still water } (u) = \frac{D + U}{2} = \frac{26 + 16}{2} = \frac{42}{2} = 21 \text{ km/h}Speed in still water (u)=2D+U​=226+16​=242​=21 km/h Speed of current (v)=D−U2=26−162=102=5 km/h\text{Speed of current } (v) = \frac{D - U}{2} = \frac{26 - 16}{2} = \frac{10}{2} = 5 \text{ km/h}Speed of current (v)=2D−U​=226−16​=210​=5 km/h

Problem 10 (Boats and Streams Time Multiple)​

A man takes twice as long to row a distance upstream as to row the same distance downstream. If the stream flows at 3 km/h, find his speed in still water.

Solution: Using the ratio shortcut: UtimeDtime=2  ⟹  u+vu−v=2\frac{U_{\text{time}}}{D_{\text{time}}} = 2 \implies \frac{u + v}{u - v} = 2Dtime​Utime​​=2⟹u−vu+v​=2 u+v=2(u−v)  ⟹  u+v=2u−2v  ⟹  u=3vu + v = 2(u - v) \implies u + v = 2u - 2v \implies u = 3vu+v=2(u−v)⟹u+v=2u−2v⟹u=3v Given v=3 km/hv = 3 \text{ km/h}v=3 km/h: u=3×3=9 km/hu = 3 \times 3 = 9 \text{ km/h}u=3×3=9 km/h The speed of the boat in still water is 9 km/h.

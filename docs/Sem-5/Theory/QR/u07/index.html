Directions & Seating Arrangements​

1. Directions Sense Reasoning​

Direction sense tests evaluate spatial orientation and the ability to trace motion trajectories across two-dimensional coordinate grids.

1.1 The Compass Framework​

There are four primary cardinal directions and four intermediate ordinal (intercardinal) directions, spaced at intervals of 45∘45^\circ45∘:

  • Cardinal Directions: North (N\text{N}N), South (S\text{S}S), East (E\text{E}E), West (W\text{W}W).
  • Intercardinal Directions: North-East (NE\text{NE}NE), South-East (SE\text{SE}SE), South-West (SW\text{SW}SW), North-West (NW\text{NW}NW).
  • Angular Separation: Each adjacent main direction is separated by 90∘90^\circ90∘. Each adjacent cardinal and intercardinal direction is separated by 45∘45^\circ45∘.

1.2 Orientation and Directional Turns​

A standard "left" or "right" turn implies an exact orthogonal rotation of 90∘90^\circ90∘:

  • Facing North: Right = East (90∘ CW90^\circ\text{ CW}90∘ CW), Left = West (90∘ ACW90^\circ\text{ ACW}90∘ ACW)
  • Facing South: Right = West (90∘ CW90^\circ\text{ CW}90∘ CW), Left = East (90∘ ACW90^\circ\text{ ACW}90∘ ACW)
  • Facing East: Right = South (90∘ CW90^\circ\text{ CW}90∘ CW), Left = North (90∘ ACW90^\circ\text{ ACW}90∘ ACW)
  • Facing West: Right = North (90∘ CW90^\circ\text{ CW}90∘ CW), Left = South (90∘ ACW90^\circ\text{ ACW}90∘ ACW)

Net Angular Rotation Rule​

When multiple rotational turns (clockwise CW\text{CW}CW and anticlockwise ACW\text{ACW}ACW) are executed: θnet=∑θCW−∑θACW\theta_{\text{net}} = \sum \theta_{\text{CW}} - \sum \theta_{\text{ACW}}θnet​=∑θCW​−∑θACW​

  • If θnet>0\theta_{\text{net}} > 0θnet​>0: Rotate clockwise from initial orientation by θnet\theta_{\text{net}}θnet​.
  • If θnet<0\theta_{\text{net}} < 0θnet​<0: Rotate anticlockwise from initial orientation by ∣θnet∣|\theta_{\text{net}}|∣θnet​∣.

2. Seating Arrangements​

Seating arrangement puzzles test logical grouping, sequential deduction, and relative positional tracking under defined constraints.

2.1 Linear Arrangements​

  • Single Row Facing North:
    • "To the right" means moving towards the East (observer's right).
    • "To the left" means moving towards the West (observer's left).
  • Single Row Facing South:
    • "To the right" means moving towards the West (observer's left).
    • "To the left" means moving towards the East (observer's right).
  • Two Parallel Rows Facing Each Other:
    • Row 1 faces South   ⟹  \implies⟹ their right is to the observer's left.
    • Row 2 faces North   ⟹  \implies⟹ their right is to the observer's right.
    • Diagonally opposite positions form an XXX-pattern between the ends.

2.2 Circular Arrangements​

Let NNN persons sit in a circle:

Case 1: All Persons Facing Inward (Towards the Center)​

  • Moving Clockwise   ⟹  \implies⟹ Moving to the Left.
  • Moving Anticlockwise   ⟹  \implies⟹ Moving to the Right.

Case 2: All Persons Facing Outward (Away from Center)​

  • Moving Clockwise   ⟹  \implies⟹ Moving to the Right.
  • Moving Anticlockwise   ⟹  \implies⟹ Moving to the Left.

Positional Mapping in an 8-Person Circle​

  • Opposite person: Exactly 3 persons sit between any pair of diametrically opposite individuals (8/2=48 / 2 = 48/2=4 positions away).
  • "Second to the right of XXX": Count two steps in the respective rightward direction from XXX.
  • "Immediate neighbor": The two adjacent slots directly adjacent to XXX.

2.3 Systematic Puzzle Solving Strategy​

  1. Identify Fixed / Definite Clues: Begin with statements that lock an absolute position (e.g., "AAA sits at an extreme end" or "BBB sits third to the right of CCC").
  2. Chain Connected Clues: Link secondary clues that reference the already placed individuals.
  3. Branch Dual Possibilities: If an ambiguity arises (e.g., "DDD sits adjacent to EEE"), draw two parallel diagrams rather than guessing.
  4. Apply Negative Constraints: Eliminate invalid branches using exclusion statements (e.g., "FFF does not sit near GGG").

3. Solved Problems and Exam Practice​

Problem 1 (Shadow at Sunrise)​

One morning after sunrise, Juhi while going to school met Anshika at a road crossing. Anshika's shadow was exactly to the right of Juhi. If they were standing face-to-face, which direction was Juhi facing?

Solution:

  1. In the morning after sunrise, the sun is in the East.
  2. Therefore, all shadows fall towards the West.
  3. The shadow was to Juhi's right.
  4. Thus, Juhi's right side points towards the West.
  5. When a person's right points to the West, the person must be facing South. Juhi was facing South (and Anshika was facing North).

Problem 2 (Displacement with Multiple Orthogonal Turns)​

Rasik walked 20 m towards North. Then he turned right and walked 30 m. Then he turned right and walked 35 m. Then he turned left and walked 15 m. Finally, he turned left and walked 15 m. In which direction and how many meters is he from his starting position?

Solution: Trace coordinates starting at (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0):

  1. North 20 m: (0,20)(0, 20)(0,20)
  2. Turn right (East) 30 m: (30,20)(30, 20)(30,20)
  3. Turn right (South) 35 m: (30,20−35)=(30,−15)(30, 20 - 35) = (30, -15)(30,20−35)=(30,−15)
  4. Turn left (East) 15 m: (30+15,−15)=(45,−15)(30 + 15, -15) = (45, -15)(30+15,−15)=(45,−15)
  5. Turn left (North) 15 m: (45,−15+15)=(45,0)(45, -15 + 15) = (45, 0)(45,−15+15)=(45,0) Ending coordinate is (45,0)(45, 0)(45,0):
  • Distance from origin =452+02=45 m= \sqrt{45^2 + 0^2} = 45\text{ m}=452+02​=45 m.
  • Direction from origin is along the positive xxx-axis, which is East. Rasik is 45 m East of his starting position.

Problem 3 (Angular Rotations)​

A man is facing West. He turns 45∘45^\circ45∘ in the clockwise direction, then another 180∘180^\circ180∘ in the same direction, and then 270∘270^\circ270∘ in the anticlockwise direction. Which direction is he facing now?

Solution: Initial direction: West (270∘270^\circ270∘).

  • Clockwise turns: +45∘+180∘=+225∘+45^\circ + 180^\circ = +225^\circ+45∘+180∘=+225∘.
  • Anticlockwise turns: −270∘-270^\circ−270∘.
  • Net rotation: θnet=225∘−270∘=−45∘(45∘ anticlockwise)\theta_{\text{net}} = 225^\circ - 270^\circ = -45^\circ \quad (45^\circ \text{ anticlockwise})θnet​=225∘−270∘=−45∘(45∘ anticlockwise) Rotating 45∘45^\circ45∘ anticlockwise from West: West−45∘=South-West\text{West} - 45^\circ = \text{South-West}West−45∘=South-West The man is facing South-West.

Problem 4 (Pythagoras Displacement)​

A child went 90 m in the East to look for his father, then turned right and went 20 m. After this, he turned right and went 30 m to reach his uncle's house. From there, he went 100 m to his North and met his father. How far did he meet his father from the starting point?

Solution: Coordinate tracking from (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0):

  1. East 90 m: (90,0)(90, 0)(90,0)
  2. Right (South) 20 m: (90,−20)(90, -20)(90,−20)
  3. Right (West) 30 m: (90−30,−20)=(60,−20)(90 - 30, -20) = (60, -20)(90−30,−20)=(60,−20)
  4. North 100 m: (60,−20+100)=(60,80)(60, -20 + 100) = (60, 80)(60,−20+100)=(60,80) The ending point is (60,80)(60, 80)(60,80). Distance d=602+802=3600+6400=10000=100 m\text{Distance } d = \sqrt{60^2 + 80^2} = \sqrt{3600 + 6400} = \sqrt{10000} = 100\text{ m}Distance d=602+802​=3600+6400​=10000​=100 m The child met his father 100 m from the starting point.

Problem 5 (Coded Directions)​

If A×BA \times BA×B means AAA is to the South of BBB, A+BA + BA+B means AAA is to the North of BBB, A%BA \% BA%B means AAA is to the East of BBB, and A−BA - BA−B means AAA is to the West of BBB, then in P%Q+R−SP \% Q + R - SP%Q+R−S, in which direction is SSS with respect to QQQ?

Solution: Decode statement by statement:

  1. P%Q  ⟹  PP \% Q \implies PP%Q⟹P is East of QQQ.
  2. Q+R  ⟹  QQ + R \implies QQ+R⟹Q is North of RRR (so RRR is South of QQQ).
  3. R−S  ⟹  RR - S \implies RR−S⟹R is West of SSS (so SSS is East of RRR). Now determine position of SSS with respect to QQQ:
  • SSS is South of QQQ's horizontal latitude (since RRR is South of QQQ and SSS shares RRR's latitude).
  • SSS is to the East of RRR. Therefore, SSS lies to the South-East of QQQ.

Problem 6 (6-Person Circular Arrangement Facing Inward)​

6 boys (Rajeev, Mohan, Vijay, Babu, Suresh, Ajay) are sitting in a circle facing towards the center:

  • Rajeev is sitting to the right of Mohan, but not just at the left of Vijay.
  • Suresh is between Babu and Vijay.
  • Ajay is sitting to the left of Vijay. Who is sitting opposite Mohan?

Solution:

  1. Fix Mohan at bottom position (6 o'clock).
  2. Rajeev is to the right (anticlockwise) of Mohan   ⟹  \implies⟹ position 5 o'clock.
  3. Suresh is between Babu and Vijay.
  4. Ajay is immediately to the left (clockwise) of Vijay.
  5. Arrangement in clockwise order: Mohan→Ajay→Vijay→Suresh→Babu→Rajeev\text{Mohan} \to \text{Ajay} \to \text{Vijay} \to \text{Suresh} \to \text{Babu} \to \text{Rajeev}Mohan→Ajay→Vijay→Suresh→Babu→Rajeev
  6. Opposite pairings across the circle of 6:
    • Mohan is opposite Suresh.
    • Ajay is opposite Babu.
    • Vijay is opposite Rajeev. Suresh is sitting opposite Mohan.

Problem 7 (4-Person Card Game Positions)​

Siva, Sathish, Amar, and Praveen are playing cards around a table facing inward. Amar is to the right of Sathish, who is to the right of Siva. Who is to the right of Amar?

Solution:

  1. Facing inward: "to the right" means moving anticlockwise.
  2. Sathish is to the right of Siva   ⟹  Siva→anticlockwiseSathish\implies \text{Siva} \xrightarrow{\text{anticlockwise}} \text{Sathish}⟹Sivaanticlockwise​Sathish.
  3. Amar is to the right of Sathish   ⟹  Sathish→anticlockwiseAmar\implies \text{Sathish} \xrightarrow{\text{anticlockwise}} \text{Amar}⟹Sathishanticlockwise​Amar.
  4. The fourth person Praveen must occupy the next position: Amar→anticlockwisePraveen\text{Amar} \xrightarrow{\text{anticlockwise}} \text{Praveen}Amaranticlockwise​Praveen Therefore, Praveen is to the right of Amar.

Problem 8 (8-Person Circular Arrangement Facing Center)​

Eight persons (P,Q,R,S,T,U,V,WP, Q, R, S, T, U, V, WP,Q,R,S,T,U,V,W) sit in a circle facing the center:

  • PPP is second to the right of TTT.
  • TTT is the neighbor of RRR and VVV.
  • SSS is not the neighbor of PPP.
  • VVV is the neighbor of UUU.
  • QQQ is not between SSS and WWW.
  • WWW is not between UUU and SSS. What is the position of SSS?

Solution:

  1. Place TTT at position 1.
  2. PPP is second to the right of T  ⟹  PT \implies PT⟹P is at position 3.
  3. TTT is neighbor of RRR and VVV. Position 2 must be either RRR or VVV.
    • Since VVV is neighbor of UUU, if VVV is at position 8 (left of TTT), then UUU is at 7, and RRR is at 2.
  4. SSS is not neighbor of PPP (cannot be at 4), so SSS is at position 6 (between UUU at 7 and WWW at 5).
  5. Then QQQ occupies position 4.
  6. Check all negative conditions: QQQ is between PPP and WWW (valid), WWW is between SSS and QQQ (valid). SSS is seated between UUU and WWW, exactly opposite to RRR.

Problem 9 (Facing Outward Seating Puzzle)​

Eight persons (L,M,N,O,P,Q,R,SL, M, N, O, P, Q, R, SL,M,N,O,P,Q,R,S) sit around a circular table facing outward:

  • PPP sits second to the right of RRR.
  • Two persons sit between SSS and QQQ.
  • QQQ sits adjacent to RRR.
  • MMM sits third to the right of LLL.
  • MMM and PPP are not immediate neighbors.
  • LLL sits second to the left of OOO.
  • The number of persons sitting between NNN and MMM is the same as between NNN and SSS. Determine the arrangement order.

Solution: Facing outward means:

  • Right   ⟹  \implies⟹ Clockwise.
  • Left   ⟹  \implies⟹ Anticlockwise.
  1. Fix RRR at position 1. PPP is second clockwise   ⟹  P\implies P⟹P is at position 3.
  2. QQQ is adjacent to RRR (position 8 or 2). Since 2 persons sit between SSS and QQQ, testing positions gives QQQ at 8 and SSS at 5.
  3. LLL sits second anticlockwise of OOO, and MMM is third clockwise of LLL. Fitting remaining slots while ensuring MMM is not next to PPP:
    • OOO sits at 4, LLL at 2, MMM at 7.
  4. The remaining slots are filled with NNN at 6, satisfying that NNN is equidistant from MMM (slot 7) and SSS (slot 5). The final clockwise order is: R→L→P→O→S→N→M→QR \to L \to P \to O \to S \to N \to M \to QR→L→P→O→S→N→M→Q.

Clocks and Calendars​

1. Calendar Reasoning​

Calendar problems rely on cyclical modular arithmetic modulo 7, termed the concept of Odd Days.

1.1 The Concept of Odd Days​

An odd day is the remainder obtained when the total number of days in a given period is divided by 7: Odd Days=Total Days(mod7)\text{Odd Days} = \text{Total Days} \pmod 7Odd Days=Total Days(mod7)

1.2 Ordinary Years vs. Leap Years​

  • Ordinary (Normal) Year:
    • Contains 365 days: 365=52×7+1  ⟹  1 Odd Day365 = 52 \times 7 + 1 \implies \mathbf{1 \text{ Odd Day}}365=52×7+1⟹1 Odd Day
    • The first day and the last day of an ordinary year are always the same (e.g., if Jan 1 is Monday, Dec 31 is Monday).
  • Leap Year:
    • Contains 366 days (February has 29 days): 366=52×7+2  ⟹  2 Odd Days366 = 52 \times 7 + 2 \implies \mathbf{2 \text{ Odd Days}}366=52×7+2⟹2 Odd Days
    • The year ends on the day following its starting day.
  • Leap Year Determination Rules:
    • Non-century years are leap years if divisible by 4 (e.g., 2004, 2024, 2028).
    • Century years are leap years if and only if divisible by 400 (e.g., 1600, 2000, 2400 are leap years; 1700, 1800, 1900, 2100 are ordinary years).

1.4 Century Odd Days​

  1. In 100 Years:
    • 76 Ordinary Years +24+ 24+24 Leap Years Total Odd Days=(76×1)+(24×2)=76+48=124\text{Total Odd Days} = (76 \times 1) + (24 \times 2) = 76 + 48 = 124Total Odd Days=(76×1)+(24×2)=76+48=124 124=17×7+5  ⟹  5 Odd Days124 = 17 \times 7 + 5 \implies \mathbf{5 \text{ Odd Days}}124=17×7+5⟹5 Odd Days
  2. In 200 Years: 5×2=10≡3 Odd Days5 \times 2 = 10 \equiv \mathbf{3 \text{ Odd Days}}5×2=10≡3 Odd Days
  3. In 300 Years: 5×3=15≡1 Odd Day5 \times 3 = 15 \equiv \mathbf{1 \text{ Odd Day}}5×3=15≡1 Odd Day
  4. In 400 Years (400 is a leap century year): (5×4)+1=21≡0 Odd Days(5 \times 4) + 1 = 21 \equiv \mathbf{0 \text{ Odd Days}}(5×4)+1=21≡0 Odd Days
  • Multiple of 400 years (800,1200,1600,2000,2400800, 1200, 1600, 2000, 2400800,1200,1600,2000,2400) always contain 0 odd days.

[!NOTE] The last day of a century can only be Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, or Friday. The last day of a century can never be Tuesday, Thursday, or Saturday.

1.5 Repetition of Calendars​

A calendar for a given year will repeat when the net sum of odd days from the starting year becomes a multiple of 7:

  • A Leap Year calendar repeats every 28 years.
  • A year immediately following a leap year (Leap+1\text{Leap} + 1Leap+1) repeats every 6 years.
  • Other ordinary years (Leap+2\text{Leap} + 2Leap+2 or Leap+3\text{Leap} + 3Leap+3) repeat every 11 years.

2. Clock Reasoning​

A clock is a circular dial of 360∘360^\circ360∘ divided into 12 hour sectors of 30∘30^\circ30∘ each, and 60 minute intervals of 6∘6^\circ6∘ each.

2.1 Angular Speeds of Hands​

  • Minute Hand:
    • Completes 360∘360^\circ360∘ in 60 minutes: Speed=360∘60=6∘ per minute\text{Speed} = \frac{360^\circ}{60} = 6^\circ \text{ per minute}Speed=60360∘​=6∘ per minute
  • Hour Hand:
    • Completes 360∘360^\circ360∘ in 12 hours (720 minutes): Speed=360∘720=0.5∘=12∘ per minute\text{Speed} = \frac{360^\circ}{720} = 0.5^\circ = \frac{1}{2}^\circ \text{ per minute}Speed=720360∘​=0.5∘=21​∘ per minute
  • Relative Speed of Minute Hand over Hour Hand: Srel=6∘−0.5∘=5.5∘=112∘ per minuteS_{\text{rel}} = 6^\circ - 0.5^\circ = 5.5^\circ = \frac{11}{2}^\circ \text{ per minute}Srel​=6∘−0.5∘=5.5∘=211​∘ per minute The minute hand gains 555555 minute spaces over the hour hand every 60 minutes (5560=1112\frac{55}{60} = \frac{11}{12}6055​=1211​ spaces/min).

2.2 The Fundamental Angle Formula​

At HHH hours and MMM minutes, the acute angle θ\thetaθ between the two hands is: θ=∣30H−112M∣\theta = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right|θ=​30H−211​M​

  • If the calculated angle θ>180∘\theta > 180^\circθ>180∘, the reflex angle is: θreflex=360∘−θ\theta_{\text{reflex}} = 360^\circ - \thetaθreflex​=360∘−θ

2.4 Determining the Exact Time of an Angle​

To find the minute MMM between hour HHH and H+1H + 1H+1 when the hands make an angle θ\thetaθ: M=211×(30H±θ)M = \frac{2}{11} \times (30H \pm \theta)M=112​×(30H±θ)

  • Use (+)(+)(+) when the minute hand is ahead of the hour hand.
  • Use (−)(-)(−) when the minute hand is behind the hour hand.

2.5 Slow and Fast (Faulty) Clocks​

Under standard correct operation, the hands of a clock coincide precisely every: Standard Coincidence Interval=360∘112∘/min=72011=65511 minutes\text{Standard Coincidence Interval} = \frac{360^\circ}{\frac{11}{2}^\circ/\text{min}} = \frac{720}{11} = 65\frac{5}{11} \text{ minutes}Standard Coincidence Interval=211​∘/min360∘​=11720​=65115​ minutes

If the hands of a faulty clock coincide every XXX minutes:

  • If X<65511X < 65\frac{5}{11}X<65115​: The clock is gaining time (running fast).
  • If X>65511X > 65\frac{5}{11}X>65115​: The clock is losing time (running slow). Total Gain or Loss in 24 Hours=(65511−XX)×24×60 minutes\text{Total Gain or Loss in 24 Hours} = \left(\frac{65\frac{5}{11} - X}{X}\right) \times 24 \times 60 \text{ minutes}Total Gain or Loss in 24 Hours=(X65115​−X​)×24×60 minutes

3. Solved Problems and Exam Practice​

Problem 1 (Finding Day of a Historical Date)​

What was the day of the week on 15th August 1947?

Solution: Break down the period up to 15th August 1947 into completed years and current year:

  1. Completed years = 1946 years:
    • 1600 years=0 odd days1600 \text{ years} = 0 \text{ odd days}1600 years=0 odd days
    • 300 years=1 odd day300 \text{ years} = 1 \text{ odd day}300 years=1 odd day
    • 46 years:46 \text{ years}:46 years: Leap years in 46=⌊46/4⌋=11\text{Leap years in 46} = \lfloor 46 / 4 \rfloor = 11Leap years in 46=⌊46/4⌋=11 Ordinary years=46−11=35\text{Ordinary years} = 46 - 11 = 35Ordinary years=46−11=35 Odd days=(11×2)+(35×1)=22+35=57≡57(mod7)=1 odd day\text{Odd days} = (11 \times 2) + (35 \times 1) = 22 + 35 = 57 \equiv 57 \pmod 7 = 1 \text{ odd day}Odd days=(11×2)+(35×1)=22+35=57≡57(mod7)=1 odd day Total odd days in 1946 years=0+1+1=2 odd days\text{Total odd days in 1946 years} = 0 + 1 + 1 = 2 \text{ odd days}Total odd days in 1946 years=0+1+1=2 odd days
  2. Current year (1947) up to 15th August:
    • Jan: 3
    • Feb (1947 is normal): 0
    • Mar: 3
    • Apr: 2
    • May: 3
    • Jun: 2
    • Jul: 3
    • Aug (up to 15): 15(mod7)=115 \pmod 7 = 115(mod7)=1 Sum=3+0+3+2+3+2+3+1=17≡17(mod7)=3 odd days\text{Sum} = 3 + 0 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 1 = 17 \equiv 17 \pmod 7 = 3 \text{ odd days}Sum=3+0+3+2+3+2+3+1=17≡17(mod7)=3 odd days
  3. Total Odd Days: 2+3=5 odd days2 + 3 = 5 \text{ odd days}2+3=5 odd days Referring to the reference code, 5 corresponds to Friday. 15th August 1947 was a Friday.

Problem 2 (Finding Day of Republic Day)​

What day of the week was 26th January 1950?

Solution:

  1. Completed years = 1949 years:
    • 1600 years=0 odd days1600 \text{ years} = 0 \text{ odd days}1600 years=0 odd days
    • 300 years=1 odd day300 \text{ years} = 1 \text{ odd day}300 years=1 odd day
    • 49 years:49 \text{ years}:49 years: Leap years=⌊49/4⌋=12\text{Leap years} = \lfloor 49 / 4 \rfloor = 12Leap years=⌊49/4⌋=12 Ordinary years=49−12=37\text{Ordinary years} = 49 - 12 = 37Ordinary years=49−12=37 Odd days=(12×2)+37=24+37=61≡61(mod7)=5 odd days\text{Odd days} = (12 \times 2) + 37 = 24 + 37 = 61 \equiv 61 \pmod 7 = 5 \text{ odd days}Odd days=(12×2)+37=24+37=61≡61(mod7)=5 odd days Total odd days in 1949 years=0+1+5=6 odd days\text{Total odd days in 1949 years} = 0 + 1 + 5 = 6 \text{ odd days}Total odd days in 1949 years=0+1+5=6 odd days
  2. Current year (1950):
    • 26 days of January: 26(mod7)=5 odd days26 \pmod 7 = 5 \text{ odd days}26(mod7)=5 odd days
  3. Total Odd Days: 6+5=11≡11(mod7)=4 odd days6 + 5 = 11 \equiv 11 \pmod 7 = 4 \text{ odd days}6+5=11≡11(mod7)=4 odd days Code 4 corresponds to Thursday. 26th January 1950 was a Thursday.

Problem 3 (Date Shift Across Normal / Leap Years)​

If 1st January 2007 was a Monday, what was the day of the week on 1st January 2008?

Solution: 2007 is an ordinary year, containing 365 days =1 odd day= 1 \text{ odd day}=1 odd day. Day on Jan 1, 2008=Monday+1=Tuesday\text{Day on Jan 1, 2008} = \text{Monday} + 1 = \text{Tuesday}Day on Jan 1, 2008=Monday+1=Tuesday

Problem 4 (Date Shift Across a Leap February)​

If 8th February 2004 was a Sunday, what day was 8th February 2005?

Solution: Year 2004 is a leap year, and the period spans 29th February 2004. Hence, 1 leap year is traversed =2 odd days= 2 \text{ odd days}=2 odd days. Day on Feb 8, 2005=Sunday+2=Tuesday\text{Day on Feb 8, 2005} = \text{Sunday} + 2 = \text{Tuesday}Day on Feb 8, 2005=Sunday+2=Tuesday

Problem 5 (Angle Between Clock Hands)​

Find the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at 4:20.

Solution: Here H=4H = 4H=4, M=20M = 20M=20. θ=∣30H−112M∣=∣30(4)−112(20)∣=∣120−110∣=10∘\theta = \left|30H - \frac{11}{2}M\right| = \left|30(4) - \frac{11}{2}(20)\right| = |120 - 110| = 10^\circθ=​30H−211​M​=​30(4)−211​(20)​=∣120−110∣=10∘ The angle between the hands is 10∘10^\circ10∘.

Problem 6 (Angle at 8:30)​

Find the acute and reflex angle between the hands of a clock at 8:30.

Solution: Here H=8H = 8H=8, M=30M = 30M=30. θ=∣30(8)−112(30)∣=∣240−165∣=75∘\theta = \left|30(8) - \frac{11}{2}(30)\right| = |240 - 165| = 75^\circθ=​30(8)−211​(30)​=∣240−165∣=75∘

  • Acute angle =75∘= 75^\circ=75∘.
  • Reflex angle =360∘−75∘=285∘= 360^\circ - 75^\circ = 285^\circ=360∘−75∘=285∘.

Problem 7 (Coincidence of Hands)​

At what time between 3 and 4 o'clock will the hands of a clock coincide?

Solution: Hands coincide when θ=0∘\theta = 0^\circθ=0∘, with H=3H = 3H=3: M=211(30H±θ)=211(30×3+0)=2×9011=18011=16411 minutesM = \frac{2}{11}(30H \pm \theta) = \frac{2}{11}(30 \times 3 + 0) = \frac{2 \times 90}{11} = \frac{180}{11} = 16\frac{4}{11} \text{ minutes}M=112​(30H±θ)=112​(30×3+0)=112×90​=11180​=16114​ minutes The hands coincide at 3 hours 16411 minutes3 \text{ hours } 16\frac{4}{11} \text{ minutes}3 hours 16114​ minutes.

Problem 8 (Opposite Hands in Straight Line)​

At what time between 7 and 8 o'clock will the hands of a clock be in the same straight line but not together?

Solution: Hands are opposite when θ=180∘\theta = 180^\circθ=180∘. Here H=7H = 7H=7: 30H=30×7=210∘30H = 30 \times 7 = 210^\circ30H=30×7=210∘ Since 210∘>180∘210^\circ > 180^\circ210∘>180∘, we take (30H−180∘)(30H - 180^\circ)(30H−180∘): M=211(210−180)=211(30)=6011=5511 minutesM = \frac{2}{11}(210 - 180) = \frac{2}{11}(30) = \frac{60}{11} = 5\frac{5}{11} \text{ minutes}M=112​(210−180)=112​(30)=1160​=5115​ minutes The hands are in a straight line opposite each other at 7 hours 5511 minutes7 \text{ hours } 5\frac{5}{11} \text{ minutes}7 hours 5115​ minutes.

Problem 9 (Right Angle Between Hands)​

At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock will the hands of a clock be at a right angle (90∘90^\circ90∘)?

Solution: Here H=4  ⟹  30H=120∘H = 4 \implies 30H = 120^\circH=4⟹30H=120∘. Right angles occur twice:

  1. First occurrence (before crossing hour hand): M1=211(120−90)=211(30)=6011=5511 minutesM_1 = \frac{2}{11}(120 - 90) = \frac{2}{11}(30) = \frac{60}{11} = 5\frac{5}{11} \text{ minutes}M1​=112​(120−90)=112​(30)=1160​=5115​ minutes
  2. Second occurrence (after crossing hour hand): M2=211(120+90)=211(210)=42011=38211 minutesM_2 = \frac{2}{11}(120 + 90) = \frac{2}{11}(210) = \frac{420}{11} = 38\frac{2}{11} \text{ minutes}M2​=112​(120+90)=112​(210)=11420​=38112​ minutes The hands form a right angle at 4:055114:05\frac{5}{11}4:05115​ and at 4:382114:38\frac{2}{11}4:38112​.

Problem 10 (Faulty Clock Gain Calculation)​

The hands of a clock coincide every 64 minutes. How much does the clock gain or lose in a day?

Solution: Standard coincidence takes 72011=65511\frac{720}{11} = 65\frac{5}{11}11720​=65115​ minutes. Since the hands coincide in 64 minutes (<65511< 65\frac{5}{11}<65115​), the clock is gaining time (fast). Gain in 64 minutes=65511−64=1511=1611 minutes\text{Gain in 64 minutes} = 65\frac{5}{11} - 64 = 1\frac{5}{11} = \frac{16}{11} \text{ minutes}Gain in 64 minutes=65115​−64=1115​=1116​ minutes Gain in 24 hours (1440 minutes)=161164×1440=1611×64×1440=144×1440=36011=32811 minutes\text{Gain in 24 hours (1440 minutes)} = \frac{\frac{16}{11}}{64} \times 1440 = \frac{16}{11 \times 64} \times 1440 = \frac{1}{44} \times 1440 = \frac{360}{11} = 32\frac{8}{11} \text{ minutes}Gain in 24 hours (1440 minutes)=641116​​×1440=11×6416​×1440=441​×1440=11360​=32118​ minutes The clock gains 3281132\frac{8}{11}32118​ minutes in 24 hours.
